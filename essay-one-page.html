The one-page memo

A memo is not an essay. It is a market decision made small enough to mark.

Five lines, in this order:

  1. The market and the question, in one sentence.
  2. The answer, in one sentence.
  3. Three facts that, if wrong, kill the answer.
  4. The loss if you are wrong — dollars, time, or standing.
  5. The action, or the explicit wait.

If line three is empty, you have a mood. If line four is poetry, you have not inventoried risk. Length past one page is usually fear of finishing.

Drill. Take a view you have repeated this month. Force it through the five lines. Read it aloud. Whatever you skip is the part you do not know.
